Your fat cells don't forget – that has now been proven.

In 2024, a research team from Zurich published a formula in the journal "Nature" that caused a sensation in metabolic medicine. The researchers studied people who had lost a significant amount of weight – through diets, some even through stomach surgery.

Their fat cells should have been functioning "normally" by now. But that's precisely what they didn't do. The cells continued to behave like the cells of severely obese people – even years later. They had effectively stored their old weight. Researchers call it a "memory" of the fat tissue. And it silently works to regain every lost kilogram.

This is how you should imagine your fat cells: The storage is "locked" – and defends your old weight against any diet.

Do you understand what this means? For decades, people like you have been told it's your own fault. Too undisciplined. Too complacent. Meanwhile, you've been fighting a mechanism within your own cells all this time – with methods that don't even touch that mechanism.

But – and this is the crucial point – this layer consists of 3 completely different layers.

And when you understand what distinguishes these 3 layers, you will understand for the first time why each of your diets ended the way it did:

  1. Subcutaneous fat. The outer layer, directly under the skin – mostly water. This is the layer that shrinks first in any diet. That's why the first few pounds always come off so quickly. But it's also the layer that has the least impact.

  2. The middle layer of body fat. Your body's pantry. This is where reserves are stored – and where, over the years, metabolic waste products accumulate, making you feel tired, heavy, and lethargic. Strict diets only chip away at this layer. Nothing more.

  3. Visceral fat – the command center. The deepest layer, tightly wrapped around your internal organs. Compact, tough, hard as a shoe sole – and practically inaccessible to diets. This layer is not a passive storage area: it sends out messenger substances that instruct your metabolism to replenish lost reserves. As long as it remains intact, your body rebuilds every layer lost – faster than you lost it.

We replicated this using data from over 9,000 overweight women and men. The pattern was always the same: diets, exercise, powders, shakes – all of these only reach the first two layers of the brain. The third layer remains untouched. And with it, memory.
